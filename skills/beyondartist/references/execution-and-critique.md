# Execution and critique

## Creation route

For substantial new art or redesign, discover the host's actual image capabilities and choose the strongest accessible **native image model first** for portraits, scenes, visual metaphors and full compositions. Compare the capabilities relevant to this brief: artistic quality and medium fidelity, reference/identity handling, generation versus editing, text handling, output size and useful control. Use exposed model options and relevant host skills; respect an explicit user model choice and authorized cost. Do not treat the latest name, highest price or a generic ranking as proof of the best fit.

If native generation is missing, or cannot perform a required operation such as reference-based editing, inspect enabled image plugins/connectors, available image skills and configured image providers next. Confirm an actual callable tool or accessible image application and the required generation/editing operation. A skill can explain a workflow without supplying a model, and image understanding does not by itself create images. Do not declare image generation unavailable before checking these routes; do not silently install a service or assume paid access.

On **Hermes**, check the active profile's configured image provider/model and exposed image-generation/editing tools separately from its conversational model. On **Claude**, check native capabilities and enabled image tools, connectors or skills, rather than assuming Claude's chat model itself renders. Apply the same discovery on Codex, ChatGPT, Gemini and other hosts. Use current tool descriptions or official host guidance if configuration is unclear; do not invent a model selector or integration. Save the chosen tool/model when exposed, its suitability and material limits in the project's production decisions, without recording credentials.

Do not retreat to SVG panels and an unchanged cutout because those are easier to assemble. Native-first routing leads to image-led artistic creation when that capability is available; exact vector work, supplied-logo placement and approved bounded edits retain their appropriate direct tools.

Before generation, confirm the designer's written handoff exists, actual foundation lessons have been translated into decisions, palette selection is justified, and the artist has compared meaningful concepts. Include the defining reference craft and declared medium from SYS-021–022: how strokes/shapes, gesture, edges, depth, material and purposeful complexity will visibly work. If any is missing, complete that step first. A minimal approved edit inherits those decisions. Create from the agreed artist brief, not directly from a competitor reference. Give the artist/image model purposeful constraints and room to interpret them. For a single thumbnail or post, make one integrated composition whose expression, type, imagery, light and color belong together. For a carousel, establish the complete narrative and visual system before rendering every requested slide; vary composition and emphasis without losing continuity.

Check output mode under SYS-018 before rendering. A fully specified brief gets A — Requested direction and B — Artist alternative; render each as a separate integrated composition, or as two complete coherent carousel sequences. Keep A faithful and preserve binding constraints in both. An explicit single-output request or bounded edit takes precedence. Three internal concepts do not imply three final outputs.

Inspect the actual generated artwork, then use the best available image editing, targeted regeneration or advanced composition to repair observed weaknesses in identity, anatomy, medium, expression or hierarchy. Combine generation with native typography, faithful logos and controlled production finishing when useful. Exact lettering and approved logos may need precise placement; that finishing should serve an already strong artistic composition. Never redraw a supplied official logo through generation when exact brand fidelity is required. Reinspect repaired outputs before delivery.

If no accessible image model can perform the task after discovery, use the strongest actual editing/composition tool and supplied assets. If that cannot provide the required visual, use SVG/native layout as the remaining executable route; an explicit vector request or bounded edit can take that route directly. Render and inspect it when tools permit. If execution is unavailable, supply an actionable editing specification: intended result, asset roles, layer order/masks, concrete edits, exact text and type treatment, color/light roles, dimensions and export settings. Include a generation/edit prompt only where it helps another available tool complete the work. Label an unrendered specification or uninspected draft accurately and state the missing capability. Do not invent a connector, model or paid-service access.

Use actual assets/tools within their authorization. Keep reference artwork and font binaries out of redistributable files unless permission is established. A generated composite is not an original documentary photograph, real product screenshot or observed event.

## Typography, hierarchy and color

Use the typography specification and intended attention path from [creative-system records SYS-009 and SYS-012–014](creative-system.md). The designer must define wording, line breaks, emphasis and spacing before production; the artist can improve the type–image relationship and hierarchy. Inspect actual pixels, not just the prompt. If the face should lead but shoe highlights or intricate texture dominate, change their scale, contrast, detail or lighting. If a punchline should lead, give the words the necessary presence. This is communication-specific, not a universal face-first rule.

Define what is read first, what supports it and what can be removed. Typography communicates mood and credibility as well as information. Choose its weight, width, spacing, scale and treatment for the idea; do not rely on a decorative font to compensate for unclear copy. Keep essential words readable at the real display size even when type is expressive, cropped or integrated into art.

Use common edges, grouping and intentional spacing. Check optical as well as mathematical balance. Give the dominant idea stronger scale or contrast than secondary information. Do not let a badge, logo or ornament steal the first read. Use the actual available font or document a substitute when editable type is required. Check multilingual glyph coverage, shaping, diacritics and reading direction; Urdu, Persian and Arabic are not interchangeable. Do not mirror logos or numerals automatically. Verify supplied wording rather than inventing text from unreadable references.

Assign color roles: dominant field, subject separation, readable text and limited emphasis. Compare what feels pleasing with what draws attention in the actual category. Premium, intelligent, energetic and urgent are intended impressions, not universal color laws. Let the message, audience, brand and contrast determine the palette. Bold colors should strengthen the focal idea; saturation alone is not stopping power. Test text over the actual image, not isolated swatches. Use labels or shape as well as color where categories must remain distinguishable.

Separate mandatory brand values from project choices and general preferences. Current explicit user direction takes precedence over earlier defaults. When the product itself must be recognized, use verified product cues deliberately instead of automatically forcing it into a creator palette. Do not repeat colors, fonts, pose or composition from an earlier rejected design as an assumed BeyondArtist house style. Artistic continuity comes from thought and execution, not identical surfaces.

When a regulated accessibility target applies, verify its current requirements and measure them; do not advertise compliance from visual judgment alone.

## YouTube thumbnails and redesigns

Build one quickly understood visual story. Keep words few and make each earn its place. The image and text should complete one another, and the thumbnail should support the video title rather than simply repeating it. Use truthful curiosity: the viewer should receive what the packaging implies.

Favor a strong focal target with a clear next read. A portrait can be that target, or can direct attention toward the idea through gaze, gesture, framing or interaction. Small screenshots, lists of UI controls and many decorative badges usually lose meaning at small size. Use only the evidence or motif needed for the story. Avoid details that resemble factual product proof when they are merely imagined.

For a redesign, compare the old and new visual story. Unless minimal changes were requested, deliver a fresh interpretation through a meaningful recomposition, recreated portrait, deliberate emotion/light, stronger hierarchy or original metaphor. Tiny movement of the same person and expression is insufficient. Recreate the face faithfully from supplied identity references; change styling and pose as the direction requires.

Inspect the exported thumbnail at a realistic mobile/feed size, such as a 320-pixel-wide preview, as well as full resolution. Check whether the hook and expression survive. Keep critical content clear of expected platform overlays, verifying current requirements when they affect delivery. A readable image is necessary but does not establish artistic quality or future CTR.

## Instagram single posts

Give one core message a strong visual hook and an immediate emotional direction. Optimize the first glance in feed, then reward closer inspection. Maintain readable type, coherent imagery and enough separation for the takeaway. A single post should not become a crowded summary of everything known about the topic. Choose elegant restraint or expressive impact according to the message; do not apply the same template to every subject.

## Carousels

Work as an editorial designer and storyteller. Plan opening promise; context/problem; explanation or evidence; progression/reveal; conclusion and an appropriate action. Slide count follows the brief. Write each slide's job and exact copy before illustration, generally with one primary idea per slide.

The cover must earn attention; the middle must deliver substance and continuation; the final slide must provide the promised payoff, summary or CTA. Vary layout—hero, contrast, diagram, example or conclusion—to create rhythm while retaining a coherent type, palette, spacing and image-treatment system. A repeated line or object can connect slides when its meaning genuinely continues. Do not withhold substance merely to prolong the sequence.

Inspect each slide alone and the full sequence. Check that later slides make sense, type remains readable and the ending fulfills the opening. Do not claim swipe or reach effects without evidence.

The source collection contains a ratio error: 1080 × 1440 is 3:4; 1080 × 1350 is 4:5. These are arithmetic examples, not current platform-upload requirements. Verify current specifications and overlays when they affect export. Screenshot safe-zone figures and reach claims are not binding specifications.

## Review as designer and artist

Render all final images/slides and inspect full resolution plus realistic display size. When A+B applies, inspect every image in both: check A against the requested structure and B for a meaningful artistic difference within fixed constraints. A missing or unrendered B is outstanding work; do not claim both choices are finished. Have the designer and artist review the same artwork when separate agents are available; otherwise perform both reviews sequentially. Each must identify any concrete weakness and necessary correction, not merely approve the other role's notes.

Reopen the library-to-decision table and compare its proposed effects with the visible result: message/hook, focal priority, reading sequence, grouping/space, typography, palette roles and art treatment. Both roles can challenge the attention order. If a cited lesson never influenced the work, correct the design or honestly revise the record; do not backfill provenance. Compare recent relevant work for unintentional repetition. Distinctiveness must come from a fitting idea and treatment, while related carousel slides preserve a coherent system. Keep intended attention and observed visual emphasis separate from untested eye movements and audience outcomes.

| Designer checks communication | Artist checks expression |
| --- | --- |
| Does the audience understand the content promise immediately? | Is there an original visual idea rather than a rearrangement of familiar pieces? |
| Do hierarchy and exact wording work at actual viewing size? | Do typography, imagery, color and light feel conceived together? |
| Are wording, implications and depicted evidence truthful? | Does the subject convincingly inhabit the chosen medium while required identity cues remain recognizable? |
| Do expression and body language support the content? | Are expression, gesture and styling natural and visually compelling? |
| Does the structure encourage the intended action without confusion? | Is the work distinctive without unnecessary decoration or borrowed composition? |
| Does a carousel fulfill its opening promise through clear progression and payoff? | Does the sequence have visual rhythm, variety and one coherent artistic language? |
| Are observed reference qualities distinguished from untested explanations of popularity? | Do the defining strokes, edges, emotion, depth and meaningful complexity actually appear, beyond palette and font changes? |

Also inspect the craft: correct names, numerals, punctuation and language shaping; credible anatomy; light/shadow consistency; deliberate materials, crop and negative space; no distracting tangencies, overlaps or awkward cutouts. Check dimensions, file type, page count/order and required transparency/color/print properties. For bounded edits, compare with the baseline to preserve unrequested content.

Apply the art-form gate in SYS-022 separately from communication and technical checks. A planned sculpture with a living face or real hair fails material fidelity even if the person is recognizable and the headline reads well. A painting needs its chosen shapes, edges and brush construction, not a photographic face covered with texture. Keep intentional expressive complexity when it carries meaning; simplify competing noise rather than flattening every work. Declare any planned hybrid and its material boundaries before creation. Do not retrospectively relabel a failed medium to excuse it. Repair the actual image and review the changed treatment; a disclosed unresolved defect is not a verified final.

Revise the actual artwork in response to the observed weakness:

- **Clear but generic:** strengthen the artistic concept or subject interaction, not merely the saturation.
- **Beautiful but confusing:** improve the promise, hierarchy, reading order or amount of information.
- **Too close to a reference:** reconsider the concept, not only the colors or font.
- **Identity drift or false implication:** correct the portrait or depicted claim before delivery.
- **Correct likeness but failed medium:** rebuild the subject through the specified material/mark language; do not preserve photographic skin or hair at the expense of the art form.
- **Reference reduced to colors and fonts:** restore the relevant expressive construction, gesture, depth or purposeful richness identified before creation, while keeping our own subject and meaning.

Render and inspect again after revision. Do not replace critique with a checklist declaration, an elaborate rationale or an invented quality score. Technical validity and artistic judgment are separate; both matter. Treat performance as something to test and learn from; appearance alone cannot guarantee virality.

## Delivery

Register actual delivered assets and review limits in the active user/project memory under SYS-023. Delivery is not user approval. Save later approval/rejection/corrections with scope and provenance.

Show the finished visual first. When A+B applies, show the requested direction first and the artist alternative second, clearly labeled; keep carousel sequences separate and explain the distinction briefly. Briefly explain the direction, what changed and why. State whether live Pinterest research, stored-library research or both were used, and whether the portrait was recreated. Mention a material limitation without burying the image under process notes. Supply appropriate exports and available editable sources second; do not overfocus on SVG, JSON or packaging details.

Never promise virality or claim user approval, CTR improvement or audience validation without actual evidence. Publishing, sending to third parties and account changes require their own authorization.

If no render is possible, a clearly labeled production specification should include exact copy, dimensions, sequence if relevant, composition, typography, color roles, artwork/portrait direction, asset needs and unresolved questions. It is unfinished production work, not a finished design. A render that cannot be visually inspected is an unverified draft.
