# BeyondArtist Design Memory — creative system

This is the canonical planning guide for message development, designer–artist collaboration and artistic hierarchy. Its stable `SYS` records are retrievable operating knowledge. Read the relevant records and cited visual studies; do not load the whole library for every task. Use [art direction](art-direction.md) for its handoff and reference-study detail and [execution and critique](execution-and-critique.md) for production and export checks.

Authority: the creator's instructions establish this workflow. Published sources support specific design principles, not the entire workflow or a promise of performance. Personal characters, outfits, palettes and poses are owner-scoped choices, never installer defaults. The library's name is **BeyondArtist Design Memory**; retain original source identifiers only for provenance and reliable retrieval.

## SYS-001 — Establish platform and purpose, then follow nine steps

Tags: workflow, intake, designer, artist, brief, platform, purpose, why, missing information, format, nine steps, observe reflect make, iterative loop

Use supplied context first. Establish **where/what** (platform and format) and **why** (desired audience understanding, feeling or action). If either is missing, ask a single concise question for the missing fields before committing the structure or render. “Where will this be used, and what should it achieve?” covers both; do not ask both when one is already clear. Continue independent memory retrieval and fact-checking while waiting. Explicit authority to choose permits a stated assumption. A vague “make it viral” expresses ambition but may still leave the communication purpose unresolved.

For substantial new design or redesign:

1. **Intake:** load applicable personal/project context, then establish platform/format, purpose, audience, language, takeaway, assets, title/copy and constraints. Ask a concise adaptive questionnaire only for consequential gaps (SYS-024), save the working brief, and classify open brief, fully specified brief or bounded edit with SYS-018.
2. **Understand and retrieve:** distinguish content category from emotional tone (SYS-017). Read relevant SYS content routes plus initial foundation and art studies; frame a provisional message, not a final look.
3. **Research:** designer inspects actual category/platform work and relevant Pinterest art before planning. Compare current examples and observed engagement where available; verify claims and preserve evidence limits (SYS-007).
4. **Build the plan:** synthesize memory and live evidence into an original angle, hook, truthful payoff, intended hierarchy, structure/sequence, typography specification and concept-led palette. Compare two saved palette directions when open; honor fixed colors.
5. **Handoff:** save the source-to-decision record and complete artist brief, including fixed instructions, creative room, output mode and visible quality checks (SYS-010).
6. **Artist exploration:** inspect the handoff and cited records; build a compact moodboard, compare three meaningfully different concepts and improve the interpretation within constraints. Meaning, concept and major shapes lead finishing (SYS-011–015).
7. **Create:** use the strongest available image-creation workflow. Produce the selected design or the required A+B choices; complete every carousel slide. A prompt alone is not the deliverable.
8. **Validate:** both roles inspect every actual final image at full and realistic viewing size. Correct text, truth, identity, emphasis, coherence and originality, then inspect revisions (SYS-016).
9. **Deliver:** finished visuals, brief rationale, honest live/stored research status, usable exports and clearly labeled alternatives. No invented performance claims.

Use distinct designer and artist agents where available, or perform the two roles sequentially honestly. The process is collaborative, not a one-way approval chain. A bounded edit inherits the existing concept and checks only affected decisions; no forced new research or alternative. Revisit earlier steps when evidence or artwork exposes a problem. SYS-020 maps this sequence to the Observe–Reflect–Make learning loop and distinguishes craft review from audience evidence; it does not impose a second competing workflow.

## SYS-002 — Turn a phrase or quote into an idea

Tags: copywriting, phrase, quote, slogan, humour, humor, sarcasm, exact copy, attribution

Determine whether the phrase is exact required text, an editable draft or merely a topic. Keep required wording, punctuation, names and attribution intact. With editable copy, propose a few concise hooks with meaningfully different emotional angles, then choose the strongest fit. Do not rewrite a verified quotation into a sharper line while still presenting it as the original quote.

Find the phrase's human situation: who says it, to whom, after what event, with what contradiction? Build image and wording as a setup/reaction or two complementary parts. For a dismissive phrase, indifference might live in posture while the world around it supplies the joke. That is an example of a mechanism, not a compulsory chair, animal or palette.

When the request is for famous or attributed dialogue, verify the words and speaker from a reliable original source where possible. Keep original jokes clearly separate from quotations. An unreadable reference caption is not a basis for inventing its text.

## SYS-003 — Make motivation specific and believable

Tags: motivation, motivational, encouragement, authenticity, self confidence, personal story, metaphor

Identify the real friction: starting, comparison, rejection, discipline, burnout or another supplied circumstance. State the useful perspective before choosing a heroic image. Prefer a specific observation or action over an empty promise. Preserve the user's tone: warm encouragement, quiet resolve, rebellious confidence and dry self-awareness suggest different art.

Explore a visual tension that makes the message felt: small action against large resistance, an imperfect object with purposeful repair, or calm self-possession amid excess. These are concept routes, not reusable templates. Humor or a character is appropriate only when it strengthens the feeling. Avoid automatic peaks, glowing brains, crowns and luxury props as substitutes for an idea. Do not fabricate the user's achievements or personal struggle to make the message more emotional.

## SYS-004 — Explain a tool or service through a relatable consequence

Tags: AI agent, AI tool, service, product, benefits, capabilities, fact checking, sarcasm, recognition

Establish what it actually does, for whom, under which limits. Verify current product names, availability and important claims before presenting them as facts. Separate supplied claims from checked evidence; do not invent prices, autonomous powers, savings or customer results. Preserve useful verified product-recognition cues while allowing an original art direction.

Translate the feature into a human task or frustration, then build the hook around the consequence. A sarcastic line can make a recognisable workplace absurdity memorable while a short supporting line explains the real function. The artwork can personify the task, dramatize the before/after or make a visual metaphor. It must not imply an unavailable capability. Do not make every tool the same robot holding a different logo. In a tool list, count actual items and resolve a title/count mismatch before illustrating.

## SYS-005 — Write the carousel as one experience

Tags: carousel, narrative, hook, sequence, editorial, slide copy, continuation, payoff, CTA

Before art, make a compact map: **slide number → role → one takeaway → exact copy → visual action → reason to continue**. The cover promises a specific useful, emotional or entertaining payoff. Middle slides deliver it through progression, examples, contrast or reveal. The ending resolves the promise; add a CTA only when appropriate. Do not force a separate CTA slide into a fixed count at the expense of requested content.

Keep one main idea per slide and remove text that belongs in the caption. Short text still needs substance: a witty headline may need one plain explanatory line. For a list, vary the scenario in ways that clarify each item. Establish recurring type roles, margins, color logic and illustration language; vary scale and composition for rhythm. Neither ten identical layouts nor ten unrelated posters form a strong sequence. Inspect every slide alone and the full sequence at feed size.

## SYS-006 — Choose the creative mechanism before the art style

Tags: creative thinking, hook, visual metaphor, character, funny, sarcastic, relatable, originality, sharing

Write the viewer's likely reason to care and one proposed reason to share or save: recognition, useful compression, an accurate joke, a surprising comparison or a personally meaningful feeling. Treat that reason as a hypothesis. “Make it viral” is an ambition to improve relevance, clarity, memorability and craft; it is not a measurable result before publishing.

Compare possible mechanisms: character reaction; object transformation; literalized phrase; unexpected scale; satirical role reversal; restrained typographic statement. Choose what expresses this message. Then propose compatible art forms. A funny character, sarcasm, 3D finish or maximalism is a choice, not an obligation. The concept must be describable without a style adjective: “a majestic character treats an ordinary chair like a throne” says more than “premium cinematic viral art.”

## SYS-007 — Research the actual viewing environment

Tags: category research, market gap, competitor, carousel research, thumbnail research, evidence, Pinterest, trends

Inspect relevant carousel, thumbnail or post examples before committing to the treatment. Save URLs/source IDs, what was visibly inspected, any visible performance signals and their limits. Study headline length, visual promise, expression, composition, contrast, repeated motifs and content substance. Views can reflect topic, creator or distribution; do not attribute them to design alone or invent CTR.

Write **sample observation → audience need → original response**. Label an inferred opportunity as a design hypothesis. If no clear gap appears, improve clarity, relevance or craft instead of manufacturing a market claim. Designer research includes relevant Pinterest visuals before final plan selection. Compare recent category examples with stronger observed engagement where visible; capture publication dates if known, review date, exact metric label and count. A large accumulated count is not a current-trend signal by itself. Rank only a defined inspected pool, and never invent unavailable counts. The artist can reuse these references and add targeted art-form exploration after handoff; avoid repeating the same search without a new question. Inspect images, not just snippets. Identify live versus stored research honestly and follow the existing authorized offline fallback when access fails. Never borrow a composition and justify it with performance.

## SYS-008 — Retrieve memory as decisions, not decoration

Tags: RAG, retrieval, design memory, library, palette, hierarchy, structure, source IDs, offline

Query **BeyondArtist Design Memory** by the decision needed, combining intent, format and mechanism: “sarcastic tool carousel hierarchy,” “quiet motivation negative space,” or “expressive Urdu headline.” Retrieve these operating records plus relevant visual studies. Begin after intake, then refine with live-research findings. Keep format, content category, tone and needed decision as separate query ingredients (SYS-017). Read full selected records before applying them; a matching title is insufficient. Use source IDs and the retrieval index for speed, not repeated ingestion of the original collection.

Record **source ID → specific lesson → decision for this brief → visible inspection check**. Cover purpose, hierarchy/structure, typography, color and art. Compare at least two saved palette relationships when color is open; explain selection or adaptation. Fixed brand colors need role/contrast decisions rather than competing brand palettes. Distinguish recorded source values from new colors. The saved knowledge works offline and is retrievable reference material, not model-weight training or proof of future engagement.

## SYS-009 — Treat typography as a major design decision

Tags: typography, textography, headline, type, lettering, line breaks, hierarchy, Urdu, RTL, readability

Before rendering, define exact copy, dominant words, supporting text, deletion candidates and intended reading order. Choose letter character for the emotion and context: authority, wit, tension or intimacy. Specify weight/width, scale relationships, line breaks, alignment, grouping, spacing and the relationship to the image. Break lines by meaning; avoid stranding a word or breaking a phrase merely to fill a box. Punctuation is part of the copy.

Decide whether type leads, the image leads or they form one immediate unit. They must not compete accidentally. Expressive lettering may carry the idea, but essential words remain legible at phone size. Check actual letters over actual imagery, including overlaps, texture, contours and highlights. Do not solve unclear copy with a fancier font.

For Urdu, check correct language, right-to-left flow, joining/shaping, diacritics, numerals and glyph coverage. Do not substitute Arabic or Persian forms casually. If generation damages exact text, use precise native typography or a supported finishing workflow. Verify spelling and punctuation after every text-related revision, not only before generation.

## SYS-010 — Give the artist a purpose and creative room

Tags: designer handoff, artist brief, collaboration, exact text, constraints, creative freedom, approval

The handoff contains: platform/format and purpose; content category and emotional tone; audience and message; exact/flexible copy; category evidence and its limits; retrieved lessons and palette candidates; proposed hook; intended first/second/third attention; structure or sequence; type direction; identity and emotional intent; format constraints; and visible success checks. Declare open-brief single output, fully specified A+B choices, or bounded edit (SYS-018). Mark what is fixed and what the artist may reinterpret; separate A’s faithful execution from B’s permitted creative variation.

Communicate what the scene should mean rather than a shopping list of props. “Self-assured character with a dry reaction to social pressure” leaves room for invention; a list of fur, shoes, jewelry and background colors does not establish a story. The artist may challenge the proposed hierarchy, metaphor, lighting, palette or type relationship. Discuss that against the message, update the brief and proceed. Preserve exact required copy, truthful claims, identity requirements and binding brand constraints. Shared decisions do not imply automatic approval gates.

## SYS-011 — Let the artist invent the visual world

Tags: artist, art direction, moodboard, concepts, art form, lighting, expression, materials, imagination

The artist reads the handoff and actual cited memories, then studies relevant artistic references. Build a compact moodboard of observed mechanisms: shape, material, camera, light, color, lettering and emotional character. Do not combine every attractive style into the result.

For substantial new work, propose at least three real interpretations that differ in metaphor, composition, emotional stance or story. Describe each idea, major shapes, text-image relationship and why it fits. Choose jointly for clarity, relevance, originality, craft and platform fit, without fabricated quality or performance scores. With a fully specified brief, honor the requested plan in A and choose a distinct artist interpretation for B; concept exploration must not erase the requested deliverable (SYS-018). Then design expression, body language, lighting, color and typography together. A character is acting in the idea; it is not an unchanged portrait placed beside a headline. Use the strongest available image-creation workflow under the skill's production rules.

## SYS-012 — Plan the viewer's intended path of attention

Tags: visual hierarchy, attention, primary, secondary, tertiary, emphasis, movement, focal point, expression

Write an intended priority for this artwork, not a claim that every viewer's gaze will follow it. **Primary** carries the immediate message or emotion. **Secondary** explains or deepens it. **Tertiary** rewards closer inspection without being needed to understand the work. These roles depend on the communication goal: a character portrait may lead with its face; a joke may lead with the punchline and use the face as its reaction.

For a character portrait, expression and head silhouette may lead; user-confirmed recognition cues can support the identity; surface detail and small reflections finish the work. These priorities depend on the brief, not a default species, body, hairstyle or palette. If bright footwear dominates a face-led concept, reduce its visual weight even if its rendering is beautiful.

Getty describes emphasis as the attention-catching part and movement as the eye's route through the artwork. Use those principles as planning tools, not audience measurement. [Getty, Principles of Design](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/principles_design.pdf)

## SYS-013 — Follow an artistic priority order

Tags: artist hierarchy, artistic priorities, meaning, emotion, concept, composition, major shapes, finishing

Use this practical order, revisiting decisions as they interact:

1. **Meaning and emotion:** what must this express?
2. **Original concept and art form:** what is our distinctive way to express it, and what medium/form language carries that meaning?
3. **Composition and major shapes:** does the idea work before fine detail?
4. **Visual hierarchy:** what deserves presence first, next and last?
5. **Expression, light, color and typography:** how do they reinforce the intended feeling and priorities?
6. **Material refinement and finishing:** which details improve the established art form without stealing its purpose?

This is BeyondArtist's working method, not a universal art-school law or a rigid one-way pipeline. Medium and material choices influence concept, major shapes, light and emotion from the start; only their refinement belongs at the end. Do not polish fur while the pose, message or reading order remains unresolved. The artist must question hierarchy when the developing image reveals a better way to communicate. The designer must welcome that improvement and check it against the brief.

## SYS-014 — Control how strongly each element speaks

Tags: contrast, stopping color, value, saturation, lighting, scale, placement, negative space, movement, balance

Use relative size, placement, spacing and grouping to make relationships understandable. A focal point need not sit in the center; balance need not be symmetrical. Apply these principles to the composition rather than treating interface examples as fixed poster layouts. [Nielsen Norman Group, 5 Principles of Visual Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/)

There is no universal stopping color. Compare value and saturation with the actual surroundings, not the hue's name alone. Reserve the strongest useful contrast for the intended focal area; preserve legibility in secondary copy. A white muzzle against a charcoal mane can help a face read, while an equally bright shoe can divert attention. These are local design judgments. [Nielsen Norman Group, Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)

Aim light and edge definition where the story needs presence. Reduce glare, overly crisp background detail or competing accent colors elsewhere. Use contours, gaze, garment lines, shapes and color relationships to connect attention targets; do not create unrelated bright straps or ornaments. Let silhouette and negative space carry the concept before texture. Getty's movement principle supports considering those visual connections. [Getty, Principles of Design](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/principles_design.pdf)

## SYS-015 — Develop a signature beyond repeated surfaces

Tags: artistic signature, originality, identity, style, silhouette, proportion, humor, variety, brand continuity

Hierarchy answers what deserves attention. Signature answers what is distinctive about the interpretation. Develop expressive timing, memorable silhouettes, purposeful proportions, visual humor and surprising relationships between image and type. Do not mistake repeated purple backgrounds, identical outfits, shoes, hands-in-pockets poses or a watermark for an artistic signature.

Compare the proposed design with recent accessible work. Keep intentional identity cues and series continuity, but re-evaluate habitual palette, pose, camera, clothing and effects. Each independent brief should earn its own flavor. A carousel needs a coherent system and controlled variation; unrelated styling on every page is not originality. Approved work can inform taste, while rejected work must not become a default.

## SYS-016 — Inspect the message and the art together

Tags: QA, critique, designer review, artist review, phone size, typography check, squint test, viral, delivery

Both roles inspect the same actual render at full size and realistic display size. Compare the intended attention order with what visibly dominates. A squint or blur check can expose competing masses, but is not eye-tracking evidence. [Nielsen Norman Group, Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)

The designer checks exact wording, truth, hook/payoff, reading order, type quality and whether retrieved lessons appear. The artist checks concept, expression, silhouette, purposeful light/color, integration, originality and the reference craft/medium checks in SYS-021–022. Both can reject misplaced emphasis. Inspect craft separately: shaping, punctuation, anatomy, tangencies, crop and consistency. Reduce unnecessary details while retaining meaningful complexity. Correct identity does not waive failed art-form fidelity; revise the actual image, then inspect it again.

For a carousel, verify sequence, sustained readability and fulfillment of the opening promise. When A+B is required, inspect both independently: A must match the detailed request, B must preserve fixed constraints and offer a meaningful artistic difference. Both must be rendered and complete; an unmade alternative is outstanding work, not a delivered choice. Deliver the finished work with a short rationale and honest live/stored research status. Do not claim virality, audience validation or improved metrics without evidence.

## SYS-017 — Separate platform, format, content category and tone

Tags: category routing, platform, format, tone, dark, meme, humor, typography, art form, retrieval

Classify four independent axes before selecting art: **platform** is where it appears; **format** is thumbnail, single post, carousel or another output; **content category** is what it communicates, such as product education, motivation, personal identity, automotive or workplace humor; **tone** is the intended feeling, such as dark satire, confidence, warmth or restrained authority. Do not confuse a carousel with a topic, a dark palette with a category, or viral with a verified result.

Honor the requested emotional world. A dark brief needs the appropriate tension, lighting, expression and type; a meme needs a readable setup/reaction and recognizable cultural context. If black/dark colors are explicitly required, preserve them. Otherwise dark subject matter need not automatically mean an all-black canvas. Choose palette roles and art form through the meaning and platform. Do not force a funny animal into serious news or generic corporate polish into requested desi satire.

Route overlapping briefs through multiple records: a sarcastic AI-tool carousel uses SYS-004 (truthful capability), SYS-005 (sequence), SYS-006 (comic mechanism) and SYS-009 (type). A Urdu meme uses SYS-002 when dialogue is attributed, SYS-009 for RTL, and relevant humor/style studies. A thumbnail also needs two to four relevant THUMB studies, regardless of topic. Search concrete English terms in local lexical retrieval; exact IDs work when the user's brief is in Urdu. Select palette, composition and artistic studies separately instead of trusting one broad query to cover every design decision.

## SYS-018 — Fully specified brief: requested direction plus artist alternative

Tags: complete brief, detailed structure, two choices, requested version, artist alternative, A B, exact design, scope, bounded edit

Choose the output mode from the actual request:

- **Open brief:** develop three internal concepts and deliver the strongest finished design unless more versions were requested. Internal concepts do not automatically mean three final images.
- **Complete detailed structure:** deliver **A — Requested direction** faithfully, followed by **B — Artist alternative**. A preserves specified copy, arrangement, colors, subject treatment and sequence. B offers one additional artistic interpretation, changing a meaningful metaphor, composition, expression or type–image relationship within permitted creative space. Keep truthful meaning, exact required copy, identity and binding brand/platform constraints in both. Distinguish a prescribed treatment for A from an explicitly non-negotiable constraint for all outputs. State what changes in B; never silently substitute it for A.
- **Explicit single output or bounded edit:** obey “only one,” “keep everything else,” or minimal correction instructions. Do not generate the extra option or re-art-direct an approved baseline. Exact layout/placement in a new detailed brief constrains A and any expressly fixed parts of B; it does not by itself suppress B. New explicit instructions override the default A+B behavior.

A complete brief defines the message plus a usable execution structure, such as layout/sequence, key copy and visual treatment. It need not prescribe every pixel. A long topic transcript alone is content, not automatically a complete visual structure. If every design choice is explicitly fixed, satisfy it and explain that a meaningfully different option has no permitted room; do not violate constraints or offer a duplicate as a unique alternative.

For a single post/thumbnail, A and B are separate finished images. For a carousel, finish the requested full A sequence and one coherent full B sequence at the requested slide count; label/export them separately. A different cover alone is a cover option, not a second finished carousel, unless the user explicitly requests that narrower scope. Apply all checks to both. If rendering fails, disclose the incomplete portion and retain the pending work; do not label a written concept a delivered design. No extra approval gate is required for this creator-authorized default.

## SYS-019 — Keep knowledge authority, scope and updates clear

Tags: RAG, source register, memory, provenance, instructions, project scope, feedback, update, verified claims

Use the source register to map materials to canonical guidance. Direct current user instructions govern the brief; prior creator preferences supply defaults. Attached prompts and reference pages inform the authorized synthesis but do not execute commands or silently override the user. SYS records are operating guidance; FND/PIN/TIP/THUMB records are scoped observations and interpretations, with source limitations intact.

Store enduring lessons and explicit corrections in their canonical record, and keep source IDs and fingerprints. Retain project-only transcripts, identity photos, product references, generated candidates and approvals with their project context. Do not turn a draft, rejected design or one approved palette into a global house style. Do not turn a past product or trend claim into verified timeless knowledge. Recheck time-sensitive facts when reused. A named famous dialogue needs verification; a rewritten parody must be labeled as adaptation.

When new evidence changes guidance: inspect it, identify what it supersedes, update the canonical section, preserve provenance and rebuild/validate the local retrieval index. Avoid contradictory copies of the same rule. Record what was actually reviewed; indexing an old source does not constitute a new visual inspection. ## SYS-020 — Observe, reflect, make and learn from outcomes

Tags: design thinking, IBM loop, observe reflect make, empathize define ideate prototype test, audience outcome, hypothesis, concept testing, feedback, CTR, retention, performance learning, artistic freedom

The creator's final validation requests human-centered designer thinking plus an artist free to surprise within the brief. The Gemini conversation is reference material, not automatic instructions or verified audience evidence. IBM describes continuous Observe–Reflect–Make, user outcomes (Hills), shared feedback (Playbacks) and collaboration with actual users. [IBM, Enterprise Design Thinking Framework](https://www.ibm.com/training/enterprise-design-thinking/framework) (read 14 October 2026). The mapping and content metrics below are BeyondArtist's adaptation, not an IBM content-creation standard.

| Thinking stage | Existing nine-step work | Decision and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Empathize / Observe | Intake, classify, retrieve, research (1–3) | Understand viewer, viewing context, need/emotion and truthful content payoff. Use actual examples and supplied audience context; label assumed motives. |
| Define / Reflect | Plan and handoff (4–5) | One viewer outcome, clear promise and testable creative hypothesis. Define the outcome before choosing the animal, palette or style. |
| Ideate / Reflect–Make | Artist exploration (6) | Compare three meaningful concepts. Designer sets destination and fixed constraints; artist invents the metaphor, emotional stance, composition and may improve preliminary decisions. |
| Prototype / Make | Concept comparison and creation (6–7) | When uncertain, test the idea with the cheapest useful rough composition, type/scale study or image draft before polish. Respect requested final output count. |
| Test / Observe–Reflect–Make | Validate, deliver, later feedback (8–9) | Inspect actual full/feed artwork, revise and deliver. When actual audience feedback or authorized analytics becomes available, revise the hypothesis and feed it into the next brief. |

Before finish, record **viewer → context → intended understanding/feeling/action → proposed reason this concept may achieve it → what would disconfirm it**. This is a design hypothesis, not a prediction. A character can target recognizability/personality; a useful carousel can target saves; a thumbnail can target qualified clicks and content payoff. Do not require five-to-ten layouts or several finished images for every task. A written concept is an internal prototype, never a substitute for a requested finished design.

Separate craft validation from audience validation. Full/phone-size checks establish observed readability, correct text, identity, hierarchy and integration. Agent critique is not real-user research; client comments are feedback, not a controlled audience test. Two generated alternatives are not an actual A/B test. Missing audience data does not block delivery or justify a made-up result.

When real metrics are supplied or read access is authorized, interpret them in context. For YouTube, consider CTR alongside impressions, comparable traffic and relevant retention/watch-time measures; higher CTR alone is not automatically better if the promise attracts the wrong audience or fails after clicking. For Instagram, consider reach-normalized saves/shares or the selected user outcome. Inspect carousel progression only if actually reported. Record source, period, metric definition and comparison limits; do not invent swipe completion, causal effects or winners from uncontrolled before/after observations. Publishing, account tests, third-party messaging and future monitoring need their own authorization.

Keep artistic invention inside the communication goal. The designer fixes meaning, truthful claims, exact required copy, identity, binding brand/platform rules and output scope. Palette, hierarchy, composition, material and metaphor remain creative unless explicitly fixed. The artist can challenge and improve them, preserving the essential promise; both roles own hierarchy and review. A clear promise can still have emotional or interpretive depth. Do not always force face → text → logo, two-to-four words, one palette or maximal simplification. Remove distractions while retaining meaningful surprise.

Close the loop with a compact project note: observation → interpretation → next experiment. Follow SYS-019 to distinguish client preference, audience evidence, project-specific feedback and reviewed reusable knowledge. No automatic publication/monitoring and no viral guarantee. A half-second response is not a universal measured threshold.

## SYS-021 — Study reference craft and preserve what makes it work

Tags: reference analysis, artistic mechanism, brushwork, strokes, mark-making, emotion, gesture, complexity, depth, edges, rhythm, keep reinterpret discard, originality

The creator's 14 October 2026 correction: a strong reference may owe its appeal to expressive strokes, emotional tension, creative construction or complexity, not only colors and fonts. Study those qualities before changing the work. Visible popularity is a reason to investigate; it does not establish which artistic choice caused engagement. Separate **visible evidence → interpretation → our decision**. Do not invent motives, inaccessible detail or performance evidence.

Inspect the actual artwork at the resolution available. Ask which choices carry its meaning:

| Craft dimension | What to study and transfer deliberately |
|---|---|
| Marks and edges | Stroke direction, weight, density and rhythm; where hard, soft, broken or lost edges model form, create energy or hold attention. Marks should do work rather than be a filter. |
| Emotion and gesture | Gaze, posture, facial tension, asymmetry and interaction; how staging, light or abstraction express a feeling beyond an added reaction face. |
| Material and depth | Surface behavior, volume, relief, overlaps, cut edges, shadows and relationships between real/imagined materials. Distinguish an intentional contrast from incoherent rendering. |
| Composition and rhythm | Major masses, scale, balance, negative space, movement, repetition and interruption; the relationship between text and image, not just where the person sits. |
| Complexity and discovery | Which layered marks, textures, symbols or secondary stories reward attention; how they remain subordinate to the central idea. Dense art can be organized, and restrained art can be expressive. |

Use **keep / reinterpret / discard** in the artist's compact working note. Keep the mechanism that serves our brief, such as directional engraving that sculpts facial planes or an asymmetric mass that creates tension. Reinterpret subject, narrative, palette, gesture and details to express our message. Discard irrelevant motifs, copied branding, captured UI and clutter. Successful relationships need not all be replaced: originality is a new authored expression, not difference for its own sake. Do not reproduce a distinctive finished artwork wholesale or treat its subject as a reusable asset.

Name **two to four defining craft qualities** with source IDs and visible acceptance checks before rendering. For example: “hatching follows cheek planes; heavy shadow masses sustain the expression; finer background marks recede.” A generic “premium, artistic, vibrant” prompt is not an art direction. If a key technique cannot be resolved from the inspected references, run a focused art-form study or a useful rough study; do not pretend preview notes reveal unseen fine detail.

Allow the artist to retain meaningful richness. Do not automatically flatten a rich artwork into a clean photo, new colors and decorative type to satisfy small-screen clarity. At phone size, check that the emotion, silhouette and main promise survive; at full size, check that the marks, materials and supporting complexity are actually present and intentional. Remove competing noise, not the artistic substance. Equally, do not add complexity to a brief whose meaning needs restraint. These are project judgments, not universal numerical rules about strokes or layers.

## SYS-022 — Transform identity through the medium and enforce art-form fidelity

Tags: sculpture, statue, marble, stone, carved, material fidelity, medium fidelity, portrait identity, likeness, bun, beard, painting, engraving, collage, hybrid, QA, repair

Choose the art form with the concept and define how it changes the subject's construction. Recognizability can come from proportions, facial structure, silhouette, hair/bun shape, beard outline and selected personal details; it does not require photographic skin and real hair in every medium. When a user asks to appear, reinterpret these cues through the chosen form. When a person/character is not required, use one only if it serves the idea; a recurring mascot must not replace stronger object, abstract, typographic or narrative concepts.

| Chosen form | What a convincing treatment must visibly resolve |
|---|---|
| Sculpture or statue | Facial planes, eyes, hair, beard and clothing follow the specified carved material, volume, relief and surface/light behavior. Veining over skin is insufficient. A bun is carved mass; a beard is carved shape and relief. Glasses or jewelry can be deliberate contemporary accents without turning the face back into living skin. Tattoos may become incised, inlaid or pigmented surface marks consistent with the concept; do not invent exact unseen tattoo artwork. |
| Painting or expressive illustration | Shapes, edges, strokes, value masses and abstraction construct form and emotion. Surface grain or paint splashes over an otherwise photographic face alone do not fulfill the treatment. |
| Engraving or ink drawing | Directional marks, line weight, spacing and shadow masses describe the subject's planes and intended feeling. Uniform automatic line texture is not enough. |
| Paper collage or mixed media | Cuts, thickness, joins, overlaps, shadows, mark layers and chosen material contrasts interact intentionally. The subject belongs in that physical/graphic language rather than appearing as an unrelated sticker. |

These are acceptance examples, not mandatory art forms or prescriptions for all collage and painting. Hybrids can be strong when their material contrast is an intentional part of the concept. State which areas use which medium, how they meet and why **before rendering**. The artist may propose a changed direction within allowed scope and update the brief before making it. A surprising result can inspire a separate deliberate concept revision, but cannot retroactively satisfy an unmet treatment in the current brief. A binding user-specified art form stays binding.

Run a distinct **art-form gate** alongside the communication gate. Compare the actual render with the selected reference-defining qualities and declared medium: (1) Is the material/form language convincing in the main subject? (2) Does its mark-making and emotional construction carry the intended meaning? (3) Is complexity purposeful and subordinate where needed? (4) Are identity cues recognizable without defeating the art form? Correct copy, dimensions, glasses, tattoos or likeness do not offset a failure here.

If an intended marble sculpture still has photographic skin, living eyes or real hair, mark that treatment as failed, repair the artwork and inspect it again. Do not rename it “human/marble hybrid,” invoke artistic freedom or write an attractive rationale just to waive the defect. Apply the same principle to a supposed painting that is only a textured photo or a collage with disconnected cutouts. Deliver only after the selected treatment and communication both pass inspection; if tools prevent a required repair, disclose the unresolved limitation rather than calling it a verified final.
