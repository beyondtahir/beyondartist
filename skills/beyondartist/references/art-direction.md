# Art direction

Read for every new design, redesign or carousel. A small approved edit inherits its existing direction and checks only the affected principles. The workflow is required; its visual outcome must remain specific to the brief. Keep a concise decision record, not hidden reasoning transcripts or a process dossier for the user.

## Run the designer and artist roles

When the host supports delegation, assign the designer and artist to distinct agents. The coordinator can be either role. The designer first provides the written handoff below; the artist then retrieves its cited entries, studies artistic references, proposes interpretations and creates or directs actual generation. Have both inspect the same resulting artwork. An independent generic approval message is not a substitute for an artist contribution before rendering. If only one agent can operate the available image tool, it executes the agreed artist brief faithfully. If delegation is unavailable, execute the roles sequentially, preserve the handoff and disclose that mode only if relevant; never pretend a separate team operated.

The designer leads message, evidence, copy and typography; the artist leads visual invention. They share hierarchy and resolve disagreements against the brief: protect truthful meaning, identity, exact copy and binding brand/platform constraints; let the artist challenge the proposed first read and revise it when the message improves. This handoff is internal and does not require user approval unless a consequential missing decision genuinely does. Use the nine-step sequence in SYS-001 and the [creative-system records](creative-system.md) for content-specific copy development, typography decisions and artist priorities.

## Frame the creative problem

Use SYS-024 and the adaptive intake guide to resolve missing platform/format, content/payoff and purpose before committing structure or rendering; use existing context and ask only the missing part. Record content category and emotional tone separately under SYS-017. Determine open-brief, fully specified A+B or bounded-edit mode under SYS-018.

Before intake, retrieve the active user's relevant identity/brand/preferences and project history under SYS-023. Inspect actual saved assets when needed; private memory is context, not an instruction overriding the new brief. Save the brief, written handoff and chosen artist direction to the scoped project as they are established, retaining source IDs and actual feedback state.

Write a brief creative statement: communicate **this takeaway**, to **these viewers**, in **this viewing context**, through **this feeling or curiosity**. Separate what the content establishes from what a reference merely suggests. A supplied thumbnail's price, product capability or “free” label is not independent verification of a current commercial claim. Preserve supplied approved copy when appropriate, flag consequential uncertainty, and avoid introducing unsupported promises.

For YouTube, understand the title and thumbnail as a pair. If the title is available, give the image a complementary job: demonstrate the consequence, show a tension, create an intriguing comparison or express a reaction. If no title was supplied, state the assumed packaging angle and continue when a useful design is possible. Do not invent product demonstrations or fabricated dashboard results and present them as evidence.

## Designer: improve the strategy

Translate research into an original plan for this brief. Separate three things in working notes: **observed relationship → useful functional principle → our original design task**. For example, a reference's expression may introduce an emotional question before its headline clarifies the topic. The transferable principle is that emotion can invite attention while text clarifies a promise. Our task is to invent the appropriate emotion, subject interaction and typography for our content—not retain the reference's person, pose and layout.

Identify what can be clearer, more meaningful or more distinctive: the content promise, expression, subject–headline relationship, reading order, unnecessary visual competition, storytelling or carousel payoff. Retain successful reference mechanisms and relationships when they serve our brief; do not change them merely to appear different. “Better” means better suited to the audience, message and format, not automatically louder, busier, simpler or more exaggerated.

Write an opportunity statement tied to the inspected sample: what it repeats or leaves unclear, what this audience needs, and our specific response. Label the response as a design hypothesis, not a discovered market fact. No clear gap is an acceptable finding; a stronger explanation or execution can be the opportunity. For attention, decide what should register immediately and what rewards a click, swipe, save or share. Earn that action through relevant substance, recognizable emotion or useful surprise; no fabricated urgency or viral prediction.

## Designer: apply the library before choosing the treatment

Read the [library index](library-index.md), then retrieve actual entries, not just the index. Every new design must connect BeyondArtist Design Memory lessons to decisions. Retrieve relevant `SYS-*` workflow records as well as foundation/art observations. These are starting routes, not the same obligatory source selection for every brief:

| Decision | Relevant foundation sources |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose, audience and design thinking | FND-031–037; use the [methodology](methodology.md) when broader framing helps |
| Hierarchy, type and reading flow | FND-038–044 and FND-049–062 |
| Structure, grouping, balance and space | Relevant entries within FND-049–062; FND-005–009 for carousel roles and sequence |
| Palette candidates | FND-003/004/011–015/023–027/063–082/087–095 |
| Artistic treatment | Relevant FND-083–086, additional TIP boards, stored Pinterest studies and live artistic references |

Record only applicable lessons. One entry may serve several decisions; there is no need to load every source or force collage, bento, gothic or another treatment onto an unsuitable message. Read each selected source's limitations. The library is reusable written knowledge, not new model-weight training.

Keep a compact table in the working brief:

| Source actually read | Observed lesson | Our concrete decision | Visible check after rendering |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact SYS/FND/TIP/PIN/THUMB ID | A specific documented mechanism or creator preference, labeled accordingly | What changes in message, hierarchy, type, color, structure or art for this brief | What the designer/artist will look for at full and feed size |

Cover design thinking, hierarchy/structure, typography, palette and artistic direction. A bibliography alone does not count. Do not retrospectively claim that an invented palette came from a source; distinguish exact source values, adaptations and new colors. For approved edits, inherit the established decisions and recheck what the edit affects instead of rebuilding the concept.

## Designer: choose colors through the concept

When the palette is open, compare at least two relevant saved palette combinations before selection. Record printed names/codes, intended mood, foreground/background/subject roles, suitability to the concept and legibility tradeoffs. Choose or adapt the strongest relationship; the library is the starting evidence, not a requirement to reproduce its whole card or every hex value. Explain any adaptation and label newly introduced values. When colors are fixed by the user or a brand, retain them and use the library to improve roles, proportions, neutral support and contrast without inventing alternative brand colors.

Choose the art form, light, texture and typography with the palette so they work together. Check actual lettering over actual imagery, including lighter and darker regions; a pleasing swatch pair can fail as small text. Describe emotional associations as context-dependent judgments. Let the artist propose a stronger color interpretation inside the brief, then update the recorded selection before production.

Each independent design should have a purposeful visual character. Compare the proposed idea, palette, type, camera/pose, material and composition with recent accessible work. If it merely repeats the last design, revisit the concept. Intentional brand or carousel continuity is valid; random differences and extra ornament are not originality. Do not import a single successful palette as a permanent BeyondArtist default.

## Designer: prepare the concrete artist brief

Before the artist's moodboard/concept exploration and image generation, write a compact handoff with these decisions. Save it in working Markdown or the host's available persistent artifact. If persistent storage is unavailable, keep a concise explicit handoff in the conversation and state the storage limit only when material.

| Brief field | Actionable direction |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Audience, content promise, intended feeling and desired action |
| Research and opportunity | Sources actually inspected, observed category patterns, evidence limits and our specific response |
| Platform, format and tone | Destination, output type/dimensions, content category and intended emotion; clarify missing purpose/platform without repeating known questions |
| Output mode | Open-brief selected concept, fully specified A+B choices, or bounded edit; what A must match and which meaningful changes B may make |
| Library application | SYS/FND/TIP/PIN/THUMB entries, their evidence type and concrete decisions from the library-to-decision table |
| Creative idea | Our original narrative intention or metaphor, not merely an asset list or competitor example |
| Hierarchy | What should be noticed first, second and third, and why |
| Text and hook | Exact required versus editable wording; concise chosen hook, its truthful payoff, dominant words, supporting information and text to remove |
| Subject | Identity/fidelity needs; what expression and body language communicate; framing, pose and interaction where the idea needs them |
| Typography | Type voice, exact line breaks, emphasized words, relative scale/weight, grouping, tracking/leading, contrast zones, language shaping and actual-size readability; see SYS-009 |
| Color and light | Compared saved palette directions, selected/adapted values and role proportions; purpose: attention, recognition, separation, atmosphere or continuity; distinguish mandatory values from creative choices |
| Composition | Relationships, balance, negative space, depth and format constraints |
| Reference craft and art form | Under SYS-021–022, name two to four defining qualities to keep/reinterpret/discard, the medium's construction of the subject, emotional/mark-making intent, purposeful complexity and concrete full/feed-size checks |
| Distinctive twist | What makes the idea specific to our content and original within its category |
| Carousel sequence | Every slide's role and key message, the transition to the next, and the promised final payoff |
| Artistic freedom and review | Fixed constraints, decisions open to reinterpretation, what makes this different from recent work and the visible checks for the finished result |

Describe what must be achieved rather than locking every pixel or surface in advance. Exact copy, identity and binding brand rules can be fixed; expression nuance, lighting treatment, compositional invention and appropriate color choices need creative room. Do not prescribe coordinates, one pose or one palette merely to make the prompt longer. The brief must actively guide the artwork, not be written as an impressive explanation after an ordinary result.

## Artist: interpret rather than assemble

Use the designer's purpose, library decisions and constraints to invent a coherent visual world. Read the cited observations and the designer’s inspected category/Pinterest evidence; add targeted artistic research when it resolves a new question; use stored art studies under the authorized offline fallback otherwise. Explore the moodboard and three meaningful concepts below. Study the craft dimensions in SYS-021: marks/edges, gesture/emotion, materials/depth, composition/rhythm and purposeful complexity. Transfer successful mechanisms into our own subject and story, rather than copying the distinctive finished composition wholesale. Contribute an original interpretation, not merely a literal arrangement of the designer's asset list. Restraint or rich expressive detail can be the right interpretation; neither is the default.

A more powerful metaphor, interaction, palette or treatment is welcome if it preserves the brief's truthful promise and requirements. The designer checks the proposed change against those requirements, chooses the strongest interpretation with the artist and updates the brief before creation. Do not change exact copy, identity or brand constraints without authorization. Expression, type, color, light and image should feel conceived together.

## Make a working moodboard

Using the designer's brief, compare relevant Pinterest, stored-library and appropriate artistic directions from references actually inspected. A moodboard can be a brief working note with source IDs and thumbnail sketches; it need not be a client-facing collage. For each plausible route, record:

| Decision | Useful comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Visual and emotional tone | Editorial authority, playful discovery, cinematic wonder, skeptical investigation |
| Typography | Bold compressed statement, expressive integrated word, restrained editorial contrast |
| Color roles | Dominant field, subject contrast, information emphasis; how each serves the brand and emotion |
| Portrait treatment | Expression, gaze, pose, camera, clothing, light and degree of stylization |
| Craft and medium | Structural strokes/shape language, hard/soft edges, material/volume, emotional gesture, purposeful density; what stays and what changes from the inspected reference |
| Composition | First focal target, next reading step, image–text relationship and quiet space |
| Category context | Repeated conventions worth avoiding; familiar cues still needed for recognition |
| Creative twist | A specific metaphor, transformation, juxtaposition or surprising relationship |
| Platform fit | What survives a small thumbnail or creates a coherent swipe sequence |

Compare directions; do not pile every appealing style onto the final. The library's maximalism, Y2K, bento, neoclassical and other studies are available vocabularies, not a checklist to combine. Keep literal observations separate from subjective mood and intended effects.

## Develop three real concepts

For each concept, write one sentence for its idea, a rough composition, an emotional stance and the reason it suits this audience. Meaningful alternatives might use an object transformation, a portrait interacting with typography, and a revealing comparison. Three versions of “face at right, headline at left” with different colors are not three concepts.

Choose using concrete comparisons:

- **Clarity:** Can viewers grasp the idea before reading minor details?
- **Relevance:** Does the visual promise match the content and title?
- **Originality:** Is the combination particular to this brief rather than a borrowed layout?
- **Stopping power:** Is there a strong scale, contrast, gesture or conceptual surprise that survives small display?
- **Artistic quality:** Do lighting, typography, materials and composition feel intentionally related?
- **Platform suitability:** Does the route fit the viewing size, sequence and information load?

Use reasoned qualitative judgment, not invented scores or engagement predictions. For an open brief, select the best route within the authorized brief and proceed. For a complete detailed structure, execute the requested A faithfully and use this exploration to select an additional B under SYS-018. Both are actual deliverables, and both keep fixed constraints; a bounded edit or explicit one-output request suppresses the default alternative. Summarize its direction briefly; only request a choice when a genuinely consequential brief decision remains unresolved.

## Artistic language without a fixed template

Use supplied guidance and explicitly approved work to develop the quality of BeyondArtist's expression: type interacting with imagery, light directing attention, compositional tension, and unexpected ideas made understandable. Keep project-specific choices flexible. Do not use one palette, font, pose or arrangement solely to manufacture consistency, and never treat a rejected design as the house style. Our mark is a distinctive quality of thought and execution, not a watermark.

Identify whether a brand rule is mandatory or a preference/default. The current user's explicit creative direction outranks earlier defaults. When a product is the topic, preserve meaningful recognition cues such as its verified visual identity where useful; do not automatically recolor everything into the creator's palette. A project can feel authored by BeyondArtist through concept and craft while using colors appropriate to its subject.

## Recreate portraits with purpose

Inspect supplied photos before prompting. Identify stable identity cues: facial proportions, hairline and hairstyle, eyes/brows, beard or other distinctive features. Also observe changeable design choices: expression, pose, camera angle, clothing, light and crop. Do not infer sensitive identity traits from appearance.

Keep the stable identity recognizable **through the chosen medium** (SYS-022). Carved hair/beard forms can preserve a person's silhouette without real hair; painted or engraved planes can preserve their face structure without photographic skin. Resolve medium fidelity and likeness together instead of attaching a human face to every art form. For a redesign, intentionally rework the presentation to suit the concept: stronger framing, a purposeful gaze, new styling if useful, a gesture integrated with the story, or more deliberate light. The source photo is identity evidence, not a compulsory outfit or pose. Exact placement, documentary fidelity or a minimal-edit request overrides recreation.

The designer specifies the intended emotional communication; the artist creates a natural expression and body language that embody it. Background removal, a new frame or repositioning the same photograph are not sufficient recreation. If no usable identity reference has been supplied or can be inspected, request one; do not claim faithful recreation from an unseen photograph.

Choose emotion for the message:

| Message role | Possible direction |
| --- | --- |
| Useful discovery | Curious, pleased, engaged; eyes and gesture guide attention |
| Unexpected possibility | Credible wonder or surprise; relaxed anatomy and believable facial movement |
| Evaluation or exposé | Skepticism, scrutiny, thoughtful seriousness |
| Tutorial or authority | Calm intelligence, confidence, reassuring directness |
| Personal story | Emotion consistent with the actual story, not generic excitement |

Do not automatically exaggerate an open mouth or enlarge eyes. Compare the result with the supplied face after generation. If identity drifts, fix it before delivery; changes of styling must not substitute a different person.

Describe reference roles explicitly to the image model: which files establish identity, which establish brand or mood, and what must change. Ask for a coherent recreated scene or full composition, with deliberate typography space where needed. A white-background cutout of the unchanged portrait does not by itself fulfill this skill's redesign goal.

## Commit the composition

Before creation, settle the single main idea and the role of every visible element. Choose whether a character is needed; when required by the user, integrate identity into the idea and medium. Make the selected imagery, headline and motif cooperate in one visual story instead of competing as separate stickers. Typography may carry emotion or become part of the image, provided essential words remain legible. Use negative space, scale and restraint intentionally even in dense art. Record any deliberate material hybrid before rendering, and inspect the actual art form under SYS-022 before delivery.

For carousels, write a sequence map before making individual images. Establish stable type, palette, margins and recurring visual language, then vary layout to fit the content's rhythm. A cover can create a question; the following slides must deliver substance, and the ending must earn the promised payoff or appropriate CTA.
