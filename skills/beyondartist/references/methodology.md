# BeyondArtist methodology

Research reviewed: **2026-10-13**. The four primary sources below were opened and read. These are compact paraphrases with practical applications; no course-completion, audience-testing, or institutional endorsement is claimed. BeyondArtist applies this knowledge through instructions and references, not model-weight training.

## 1. Understand the problem before choosing a style

**Source principle.** Design Council's Double Diamond separates broad exploration from focused selection, first for the problem and then for solutions. Its four stages are Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver, with iteration when new evidence changes the brief. [Design Council, Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/) (accessed 2026-10-13).

**BeyondArtist application.** Record the recipient, setting, message, action and constraints before designing. Distinguish supplied evidence from assumptions. Define one communication problem in a sentence. Develop different explanations or metaphors, not merely different colors. Compare rough concepts before investing in finish. If a visually attractive draft obscures the message, revisit the concept instead of adding decoration. For an approved-design correction, apply this process only to the requested change.

**Decision gate:** Can the brief state who should understand what, where they will see it, and what they should feel or do? If missing information could reverse the design choice, ask for it; otherwise use a labeled, reversible assumption.

## 2. Design from the recipient's situation

**Source principle.** IDEO describes human-centered design as starting from people's needs, experiences and contexts; design thinking applies that orientation through exploration, prototypes and iteration. Its three lenses consider whether a solution matters to people, can be made, and can work within real constraints. [IDEO, Introduction to Design Thinking](https://designthinking.ideo.com/introduction) (accessed 2026-10-13).

**BeyondArtist application.** Check language, reading direction, cultural associations, prior knowledge, accessibility needs and viewing distance. A luxury metaphor may be inappropriate for a practical public-service notice. A small mobile graphic needs less copy than a poster examined closely. Judge concepts for audience relevance, understandable meaning and feasible execution with the actual tools, assets and time available. Invite concrete client or audience feedback when available and revise against it; an agent's simulated critique is not audience research.

**Decision gate:** Identify the assumption most likely to make the design fail, then use available evidence or a small prototype to reduce that uncertainty.

## 3. Make meaning with image, type, shape and composition

**Source principle.** CalArts' published Fundamentals of Graphic Design curriculum connects imagemaking, typography, shape/color and composition. It distinguishes literal image content from associated meaning and covers spacing, figure/ground, rhythm, cropping, hierarchy and context. The public curriculum was reviewed; its course videos and assignments were not completed. [CalArts / Michael Worthington, Fundamentals of Graphic Design](https://www.coursera.org/learn/fundamentals-of-graphic-design) (accessed 2026-10-13).

**BeyondArtist application.** For imagery, identify both what is shown and what it may suggest to this audience. Choose a photographic, illustrative, symbolic or typographic treatment for its communication role. Make type expressive only while keeping the required message readable. Establish a repeatable spacing/grid system, then use scale, cropping and negative space to create emphasis. Assign each palette color a role. In a carousel, repeat the visual grammar while varying composition to match explanation, evidence and conclusion.

**Decision gate:** Each conspicuous treatment should have a purpose—emphasis, grouping, meaning, pacing or emotion. Remove treatments that compete with the intended takeaway.

## 4. Diagnose the composition using visible evidence

**Source principle.** Nielsen Norman Group identifies scale, visual hierarchy, balance, contrast and Gestalt grouping as practical visual-design principles. Their article specifically cautions that reducing text contrast can sacrifice legibility. Its advice is situated in UX; transferring it to graphic design is BeyondArtist's application, not a claim that all media behave identically. [Kelley Gordon, 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/) (published 2020-03-01; last reviewed 2026-09-01; accessed 2026-10-13).

**BeyondArtist application.** Inspect the actual render:

- **Scale/hierarchy:** Name the first, second and third intended reading targets. Remove competing emphasis.
- **Balance:** Compare the visual weight of text, imagery and empty space; deliberate asymmetry must still support reading.
- **Contrast:** Check text against the actual background, including gradients, photos and export artifacts. Differentiate categories with more than color alone when needed.
- **Grouping:** Use proximity and consistent treatment to show what belongs together; increase separation between unrelated items.

**Decision gate:** Describe an observable problem and its correction. Do not convert subjective judgment into invented quality scores or performance claims.

## BeyondArtist operational conventions

These conventions come from the supplied brief and this skill's workflow design, not from requirements imposed by the four sources:

1. Develop three meaningfully different concepts for substantial new work. Compare audience fit, clarity, originality and feasibility; provide a short selection rationale.
2. Retrieve a few pertinent library entries by format, audience, purpose and visual mechanism. Separate observed features from inferred effects, and inspiration from binding brand rules.
3. Plan carousel opening, progression, transitions and conclusion before individual slides. Each slide must advance the message.
4. Use a strong available image model for artistic creation and recreation, then combine with advanced visual tools for precise finishing where useful. Check every generated word. Use native text layers when needed for accuracy, without substituting layout assembly for the creative work. SVG/layout-only creation is a fallback unless the user specifically requested vector work or a bounded native edit.
5. Render and inspect every final page at both full resolution and realistic viewing size. Verify exact text, dimensions, crop, spacing and overlaps; revise observable weaknesses.
6. Lead with the finished visual and concise creative reasoning; provide available editable sources or export options second. If the host cannot render or inspect, clearly label the result as a production specification or unverified draft and state the remaining step.

## Reference-analysis record

Use this compact structure for new entries:

`ID | source URL/path | access date | format | intended audience (known/inferred) | purpose | observed palette/type/grid/imagery | inferred attention/meaning/emotion mechanism | useful context | risks/limits | permission status | review status`

Example of the distinction: **Observation:** a single oversized verb appears above two short explanatory lines. **Interpretation:** the scale likely makes the action legible during quick scanning. **Limit:** no audience or performance evidence was provided. Never label the interpretation as measured effectiveness.
